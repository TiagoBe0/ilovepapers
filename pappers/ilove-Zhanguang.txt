Keywords:                                                High-entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted significant attention due to their exceptional mechanical properties
High-entropy alloys                                      and remarkable radiation resistance. However, structural materials in reactors are subjected not only to irra­
Irradiation                                              diation damage but also to fatigue loads induced by thermal cycling and vibration during their service life. In this
Cyclic deformation
                                                         study, we investigate the mechanical response and microstructural evolution of the FeCoNiCrCu HEA under
Molecular dynamics
                                                         cyclic loading after cascade overlapping simulations, using molecular dynamics simulations. The irradiation
                                                         process was simulated at various primary knock-on atom energies up to 20 keV to introduce defect structures.
                                                         Our simulations reveal the specific atomistic mechanisms driving the cyclic hardening behavior in HEAs: lattice
                                                         disordered atoms play a dual role, acting as localized stress concentrators for dislocation nucleation while
                                                         simultaneously serving as strong pinning sites that contribute to the initial yield strength. As cyclic deformation
                                                         progresses, the evolution of the dislocation network governs the global transition between softening and hard­
                                                         ening. In the later stages of cycling, a surge in stacking fault fraction is observed, which is identified as the
                                                         dominant mechanism driving the sustained cyclic hardening. These findings provide atomic-scale insights into
                                                         the correlation between irradiation defects and fatigue performance in HEAs. While the high strain rate may bias
                                                         dislocation and stacking-fault accumulation quantitatively, the qualitative mechanisms identified remain robust.
